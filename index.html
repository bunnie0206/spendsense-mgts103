The Psychology of Spending

A statistical analysis of consumer shopping behaviour, exploring how age, discounts, product categories and seasons relate to spending.

Research question

What factors are associated with consumer spending, and how can retailers use these findings to improve their marketing strategies?

Audience: retail businesses and marketing managers. This exploratory analysis uses synthetic data, not measured psychological traits or real transactions.

Methods

Descriptive statistics, sample standard deviation (n−1), inclusive quartiles, frequency counts, arithmetic group means, percentages and Pearson correlation. Group comparisons are descriptive, not significance tests. All monetary values are USD.

Fields used: age (years), purchase amount (USD), discount status (yes/no), category (nominal), season (nominal), customer ID (identifier).

Recommendations

Segment beyond age

With r near zero and similar age-group means, test behavioural segmentation rather than relying on age alone.

Test promotional effectiveness

The $0.85 gap is small and does not establish a discount effect. Test promotions using real conversion, basket value and margin data.

Explore category and seasonal campaigns

Explore clothing and accessories based on sample volume, not profitability. Seasonal differences are small and exploratory; validate campaigns using real sales.

Limitations

The data are synthetic and not representative evidence about actual consumers. No dates, profit, conversion rates or direct psychological measures are available. Associations do not establish causation; group composition and other variables may confound comparisons. Validate all recommendations with real business data.
